## How To Write Proposals

Read `references/proposal-system.md` for the full proposal writing system, templates, and examples.

### Core Principles (Always Follow)

1. **Open with the client's problem, not your credentials.** The first sentence should make the client feel understood, not impressed.

2. **Show, don't claim.** Never say "I'm an expert in X." Instead: "I built [specific product] that does [exactly what they need]. Here's the link."

3. **One relevant product, described specifically.** Don't list all 4 products. Pick the ONE most relevant to their job, describe it in 2-3 sentences showing it solves a similar problem, and link to it.

4. **Anticipate their architecture.** If they describe their problem, sketch how you'd approach it in 2-3 bullet points. This is what separates a $100/hr freelancer from a $30/hr one — you're already thinking about their system.

5. **Close with a concrete next step.** Not "let me know if you're interested." Instead: "Happy to jump on a 15-minute call this week to walk through how I'd approach this. I can also share a brief technical outline before we talk."

6. **Keep it under 200 words.** Upwork proposals are scanned, not read. Density wins.

7. **Never mention Webskitters.** That history is background noise. Pedro is a solo AI product builder.

8. **Use the commercial instinct.** Pedro's sales background means he understands client psychology. Proposals should feel consultative, not transactional.

9. **Match the client's register.** Startup founder writing casually? Match it. Enterprise PM writing formally? Match that. Mirror their energy.

10. **Trilingual advantage.** If the client or job post is in Portuguese or Spanish, mention fluency. For Latin American or Iberian clients, consider writing the proposal in their language.

### Proposal Structure Template

```
[1-2 sentences showing you understand their specific problem]

[2-3 sentences about the ONE most relevant product you've built and how it maps to their need — include link]

[2-3 bullet points sketching your approach to their specific project]

[1 sentence concrete next step]
```

### What To NEVER Include In Proposals

- Generic greetings ("Dear Hiring Manager")
- "I am writing to express my interest..."
- Listing every skill or technology you know
- Mentioning all 4+ products
- Webskitters or any agency association
- "I am a hard worker" or any empty claim
- Asking the client to repeat information already in the job post
- Price before they've asked (unless it's a fixed-price job)

- For annotation/RLHF work on Upwork (rare, but exists): $40-55/hr is competitive for someone with Pedro's technical depth. Most annotators are non-technical.

## Bidding Strategy

### Phase 1: Build AI Reviews (First 2-4 Weeks)

- **Goal:** Get 3-5 completed jobs with 5-star reviews that mention AI/LLM/Claude.
- **Tactic:** Bid on 10-15 jobs per week. Target smaller projects ($200-$2,000) that can be completed in 1-2 weeks.
- **Job types to hunt:**
  - "Add ChatGPT / Claude to my app"
  - "Build a chatbot for my website"
  - "Prompt engineering for my AI product"
  - "Scrape X and analyze with AI"
  - "Build me a simple AI tool / MVP"
- **Rate:** Slightly below target. Speed of review accumulation matters more than per-job revenue.

### Phase 2: Raise Rates & Target Premium (Weeks 4-8)

- **Goal:** Land 2-3 larger contracts ($3K-$15K) at target rates.
- **Tactic:** Bid on fewer jobs (5-8/week) but spend more time per proposal. Custom proposals only.
- **Job types to hunt:**
  - "AI-powered SaaS MVP"
  - "Integrate LLM into our platform"
  - "Rebuild our AI pipeline"
  - "Technical co-founder for AI startup" (equity gigs — be selective)
- **Rate:** Full target rate. Let the reviews justify it.

### Phase 3: Inbound & Repeat Clients (Weeks 8+)

- **Goal:** Shift from bidding to being invited. Build a roster of repeat clients.
- **Tactic:** Ask every completed client for a review. Maintain profile with weekly bidding even when busy.
- **Rate:** Move toward ceiling as inbound grows.

### Jobs To Avoid

- "Build me a WordPress site" — wrong positioning, anchors profile to old identity
- "I need 1000 pages annotated" — volume annotation work is a race to the bottom
- "Copy this app" — clone jobs signal price-sensitive clients
- Any job that says "budget: $50-$100" for real development work
- Jobs with 20+ proposals already — low probability of being seen
- Clients with no payment history and vague descriptions

## Responding To Client Messages

### After Getting Hired

- Send a kickoff message within 2 hours. Outline: what you'll deliver, timeline, what you need from them, when you'll check in next.
- Use their name. Sound human. No corporate template language.

### During The Project

- Send unprompted progress updates every 2-3 days. Clients on Upwork rate "communication" heavily.
- If you're blocked waiting on them, say so clearly but politely: "I'm ready to move to [next step] — just need [X] from you when you get a chance."
- Share Loom/video walkthroughs for deliverables. This consistently generates 5-star reviews.

### Asking For Reviews

- At project close, send: "Really enjoyed working on this — if you're happy with the result, a quick review mentioning [specific thing you did] would mean a lot. Helps other clients find me for similar work."
- Be specific about what to mention. "If you could mention the Claude integration work" primes the review content for your positioning.

## Upwork Algorithm & Profile Optimisation

### How Upwork Search Works

- **Recency matters.** Active profiles (bidding, completing jobs, updating profile) rank higher.
- **JSS is king.** Pedro's 98% is elite. Protect it — never take a job you think will end badly.
- **Relevance matching.** Upwork matches job keywords to profile title, skills tags, and bio. This is why the skills tag swap matters.
- **Response time.** Faster proposal submissions rank higher. Bid within the first 24 hours of a job posting.
- **Earnings velocity.** Accounts earning consistently rank higher than dormant ones.

### Profile Maintenance Cadence

- **Weekly:** Bid on at least 3-5 jobs even when busy. Keeps the profile active in Upwork's algorithm.
- **Monthly:** Update portfolio screenshots if products have changed. Refresh bio if you've shipped something new.
- **After every completed job:** Ensure the review is in. Follow up if the client hasn't left one within a week.
- **Quarterly:** Review specialized profiles. Adjust titles and bios based on which jobs are converting.

### Connects Strategy

- Upwork charges connects per bid. Don't waste them on low-probability jobs.
- Prioritize: jobs posted <24 hours ago, <10 proposals, payment verified, clear scope.
- Boosted proposals (extra connects for top placement): only use for jobs above $2K where you're a perfect fit.
